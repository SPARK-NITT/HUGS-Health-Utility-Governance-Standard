# Terminology

## Medicine

The clinical activity of protecting, maintaining, restoring, or improving human health.

## Medical industry

The financial, administrative, institutional, technological, insurance, ownership, logistics, pharmaceutical, and commercial machinery surrounding medicine.

## Patient

A person seeking or receiving medically necessary care. Consumer protections may also apply, but consumer status does not extinguish clinical obligations.

## Medically necessary care

Care that is clinically relevant to protecting, maintaining, restoring, or improving health, or preventing foreseeable harm, within the judgment framework of competent clinical authority.

## Excellence

Reward derived from demonstrated competence, patient outcomes, research, teaching, difficult specialization, leadership, innovation, reliability, and service.

## Extraction

Revenue optimization that depends materially upon illness, fragmentation, opacity, unnecessary repetition, administrative obstruction, preventable dependency, or information asymmetry.

## Care gap

A foreseeable failure in care continuity where responsibility is unassigned, lost, rejected, or unverified.

## CARE_CONTINUITY_RECORD

A persistent patient-episode governance object that tracks the longitudinal care trajectory until clinical closure or formally accepted transfer.

## CARE_TRANSITION

A discrete event object that records transfer of responsibility between entities, teams, or stages of care and references the associated episode.
