0. REPOSITORY IDENTITY
======================================================================

Repository Name:
HUGS-Health-Utility-Governance-Standard

Canonical Expansion:
H.U.G.S. = Health Utility Governance Standard

Working Tagline:
Healthcare is essential civic infrastructure. A person receiving medically necessary care is a patient first, not merely a customer.

Core Human Principle:
A healthcare system should be organized around care for human beings. The word “HUGS” is intentionally fitting: a hug represents love, care, protection, reassurance, and human concern. The governance architecture underneath that name should be rigorous enough to make those values operational rather than sentimental.

Repository Purpose:
Create an open governance standard for reclassifying and governing medically necessary healthcare as an essential public-service function, whether delivered by public, nonprofit, private, cooperative, academic, or hybrid institutions.

H.U.G.S. does NOT require:
- Government ownership of all healthcare delivery.
- Equal compensation for all clinicians.
- Elimination of private medical practice.
- Elimination of innovation, competition, or professional advancement.
- A single national financing model.

H.U.G.S. DOES require:
- Patient status to take priority over consumer status in medically necessary care.
- Clinical purpose to take priority over extractive revenue optimization.
- Continuity of care to be treated as a system obligation.
- Medical authority to be protected from inappropriate financial override.
- Pricing, denial, ownership, referral, and performance systems to be auditable.
- Excellence to be rewarded without making illness itself the extraction mechanism.
- Essential-care access to be governed by public-service duties.

======================================================================
1. FOUNDATIONAL PROBLEM STATEMENT
======================================================================

Observed failure pattern:

Modern healthcare systems can convert the patient relationship into a transactional pipeline:

Customer acquisition
→ appointment
→ billable encounter
→ referral
→ another billable encounter
→ automated follow-up
→ repeated transactions

while no single institution retains responsibility for the patient’s complete healing trajectory.

The intended clinical relationship is:

Patient
→ physician / care team
→ diagnosis
→ treatment
→ recovery / stabilization
→ continuity
→ prevention

H.U.G.S. treats the divergence between these two chains as a governance problem.

Primary question:
What is healthcare before we ask who pays for it?

H.U.G.S. answer:
Medically necessary healthcare is essential civic infrastructure whose economic mechanisms must remain subordinate to its clinical and human obligations.

======================================================================
2. FOUNDATIONAL DISTINCTIONS
======================================================================

2.1 MEDICINE
The clinical activity of protecting, maintaining, restoring, or improving human health.

2.2 MEDICAL INDUSTRY
The financial, administrative, institutional, technological, insurance, ownership, logistics, pharmaceutical, and commercial machinery surrounding medicine.

Governance rule:
The medical industry exists in service of medicine.
Medicine does not exist in service of the medical industry.

2.3 EXCELLENCE VS. EXTRACTION

Excellence:
Reward derived from demonstrated competence, patient outcomes, research, teaching, difficult specialization, leadership, innovation, reliability, and service.

Extraction:
Revenue optimization that depends materially upon illness, fragmentation, opacity, unnecessary repetition, administrative obstruction, preventable dependency, or information asymmetry.

Canonical doctrine:
EXCELLENCE MAY BE REWARDED.
ILLNESS MAY NOT BE GOVERNED AS AN EXTRACTION OPPORTUNITY.

3. CORE H.U.G.S. DOCTRINES
======================================================================

HUGS-D01 — PATIENT STATUS DOCTRINE
A person seeking or receiving medically necessary care is a patient first.
Consumer status may provide additional protections but may not extinguish clinical obligations.

HUGS-D02 — ESSENTIAL SERVICE DOCTRINE
Medically necessary care is an essential-service function.
Organizations that provide it accept obligations exceeding those of ordinary commercial vendors.

HUGS-D03 — CLINICAL PRIMACY DOCTRINE
Financial, administrative, ownership, insurance, scheduling, or algorithmic systems may not improperly override medically justified clinical decisions.

HUGS-D04 — CONTINUITY OF CARE DOCTRINE
The care system must preserve responsibility across transitions.
A successful encounter does not constitute successful healthcare when the patient’s larger healing trajectory fails.

HUGS-D05 — EXCELLENCE WITHOUT EXTRACTION DOCTRINE
Clinical excellence may produce greater compensation, status, authority, resources, and opportunity.
Revenue extraction from medical necessity may not become the dominant optimization objective.

HUGS-D06 — TRANSPARENCY DOCTRINE
Patients and regulators must be able to understand material pricing, ownership, denial, referral, incentive, and conflict-of-interest structures affecting care.

HUGS-D07 — NON-ABANDONMENT DOCTRINE
Administrative boundaries, ownership transfers, insurance transitions, discharge events, or referral events may not create an unowned clinical gap when foreseeable harm can result.

HUGS-D08 — HUMAN AUTHORITY DOCTRINE
Automated systems may assist but may not silently become the final authority over consequential clinical access, denial, discharge, triage, or continuation decisions without accountable human review.

HUGS-D09 — UNIVERSAL SERVICE OBLIGATION
A compliant healthcare system must define a baseline of medically necessary care that is available irrespective of the profitability of the individual patient encounter.

HUGS-D10 — PUBLIC ACCOUNTABILITY DOCTRINE
Entities exercising authority over essential healthcare functions incur heightened auditability and accountability obligations proportional to the power they exercise over patient outcomes.

4. GOVERNANCE PILLARS
======================================================================

PILLAR 1 — PATIENT SOVEREIGNTY
Goal:
Protect the human being from being reduced to a revenue object, account number, insurance class, or scheduling unit.

Controls:
- Patient-first classification.
- Clear explanation of care pathways.
- Access to records.
- Explanation of denials and delays.
- Human review of consequential automation.
- Continuity ownership.

PILLAR 2 — CLINICAL AUTHORITY
Goal:
Protect legitimate medical judgment from inappropriate economic override.

Controls:
- Documented clinical rationale.
- Override logging.
- Denial attribution.
- Conflict-of-interest disclosure.
- Escalation pathways.
- Independent clinical review.

PILLAR 3 — CONTINUITY
Goal:
Prevent the patient from falling between organizations or phases of care.

Controls:
- Named transition owner.
- Closed-loop referrals.
- Discharge handoff verification.
- Medication reconciliation.
- Rehab / post-acute handoff.
- Follow-up confirmation.
- Escalation for missed care milestones.

PILLAR 4 — TRANSPARENT ECONOMICS
Goal:
Permit sustainable healthcare economics without hidden extraction.

Controls:
- Material price disclosure.
- Patient liability estimates.
- Incentive disclosure.
- Ownership transparency.
- Referral compensation disclosure.
- Billing-event traceability.
- Anti-fragmentation analysis.

PILLAR 5 — EXCELLENCE
Goal:
Create a system where better care produces greater authority and recognition without requiring greater exploitation.

Controls:
- Outcome-aware credentialing.
- Teaching and mentoring credit.
- Difficult-case recognition.
- Research and innovation credit.
- Patient safety performance.
- Continuity performance.
- Peer-reviewed authority.

PILLAR 6 — PUBLIC-SERVICE DUTY
Goal:
Attach essential-service obligations to institutions exercising medical power.

Controls:
- Baseline access duties.
- Emergency obligations.
- Non-discrimination.
- Community service expectations.
- Resilience requirements.
- Auditability.
- Public reporting.

5. H.U.G.S. AUTHORITY MODEL
======================================================================

Define five authority classes:

A0 — PATIENT AUTHORITY
Rights involving consent, bodily autonomy, information, records, preferences, and participation.

A1 — CLINICAL AUTHORITY
Licensed clinical judgment over diagnosis, treatment, contraindication, urgency, and medical necessity.

A2 — OPERATIONAL AUTHORITY
Scheduling, staffing, routing, logistics, facility management, and resource coordination.

A3 — FINANCIAL AUTHORITY
Payment rules, budgeting, reimbursement, contracting, insurance, utilization management.

A4 — OVERSIGHT AUTHORITY
Audit, accreditation, ethics, patient safety, public regulation, independent review.

Canonical precedence rule:

A3 MUST NOT silently supersede A1.

Any consequential A3 → A1 override must:
1. be explicit,
2. identify the accountable decision-maker,
3. state the rule invoked,
4. record the clinical consequence,
5. provide appeal or escalation,
6. be auditable by A4.

8. INITIAL CONTROL CATALOG
======================================================================

HUGS-C001 — Patient-First Classification
Doctrine: HUGS-D01
Requirement:
Policies, workflows, contracts, and automated systems must not reduce medically necessary care to an ordinary consumer transaction where doing so weakens clinical duties.

HUGS-C002 — Named Clinical Owner
Doctrine: HUGS-D04
Requirement:
Every active episode of significant care must identify a clinician or accountable clinical team responsible for the current care trajectory.

HUGS-C003 — Closed-Loop Referral
Doctrine: HUGS-D04
Requirement:
A referral is not complete when it is sent. It is complete when receipt is confirmed, responsibility is accepted, and material clinical information is transferred.

HUGS-C004 — Discharge Continuity Verification
Doctrine: HUGS-D04 / HUGS-D07
Requirement:
Discharge must include verified next-step ownership when foreseeable harm could result from a gap.

HUGS-C005 — Financial Override Logging
Doctrine: HUGS-D03
Requirement:
Any consequential financial or administrative override of a clinician’s recommendation must produce an auditable record.

HUGS-C006 — Consequential Automation Review
Doctrine: HUGS-D08
Requirement:
Automated denial, triage, discharge, authorization, or access decisions with material health consequences must provide accountable human review.

HUGS-C007 — Material Price Transparency
Doctrine: HUGS-D06
Requirement:
Patients must receive understandable material financial information before non-emergency care when reasonably possible.

HUGS-C008 — Ownership and Incentive Disclosure
Doctrine: HUGS-D06
Requirement:
Material ownership, referral, compensation, or incentive relationships that could affect care pathways must be discoverable.

HUGS-C009 — Care Gap Escalation
Doctrine: HUGS-D07
Requirement:
Detected care gaps must automatically enter an escalation process rather than merely producing an administrative notification.

HUGS-C010 — Essential Access Floor
Doctrine: HUGS-D09
Requirement:
Participating institutions must define and honor minimum essential-service duties independent of the profitability of individual encounters.

HUGS-C011 — Excellence Attribution
Doctrine: HUGS-D05
Requirement:
Institutional advancement systems should recognize clinical outcomes, difficult-case competence, safety, continuity, teaching, research, and peer contribution rather than rely predominantly on revenue production.

HUGS-C012 — Encounter Fragmentation Review
Doctrine: HUGS-D05
Requirement:
Organizations must be capable of detecting whether operational or financial structures incentivize unnecessary fragmentation of a clinically coherent episode into multiple revenue events.

9. COMPLIANCE MODEL
======================================================================

Suggested dimensions:

P = Patient Protection
C = Clinical Primacy
T = Continuity
E = Economic Transparency
X = Excellence Integrity
A = Accountability

Each dimension scored 0.00 to 1.00 from evidence-backed controls.

Initial composite:

HUGS_SCORE =
wP(P) + wC(C) + wT(T) + wE(E) + wX(X) + wA(A)

Default weights for prototype only:
wP = 0.20
wC = 0.20
wT = 0.20
wE = 0.15
wX = 0.10
wA = 0.15

Sum = 1.00

IMPORTANT:
These numbers are provisional engineering values, not moral constants.
The repository must distinguish:
- normative doctrine,
- measurement design,
- empirical calibration.

No institution may obtain “compliant” status if a critical floor fails, regardless of aggregate score.

Critical-floor examples:
- abandonment,
- unsafe discharge,
- unreviewable consequential denial,
- falsified records,
- systematic discriminatory access,
- unresolved catastrophic care gaps.

Proposed status bands:

0.90–1.00  Exemplary
0.80–0.89  Compliant
0.65–0.79  Conditional
0.50–0.64  Deficient
<0.50      Noncompliant

Hard-stop rule:
Any designated CRITICAL control failure overrides the numeric band.

12. README OPENING SEED
======================================================================

# H.U.G.S. — Health Utility Governance Standard

Healthcare is not merely a marketplace.

When a human being becomes sick, injured, disabled, medically unstable, or dependent upon professional care, the relationship created is fundamentally different from an ordinary commercial transaction.

H.U.G.S. is an open governance standard for treating medically necessary healthcare as essential civic infrastructure.

Its central principle is simple:

A patient is a patient first.

H.U.G.S. does not require all healthcare providers to become government employees, nor does it prohibit clinicians or institutions from earning sustainable compensation. It establishes instead that economic activity surrounding medicine must remain subordinate to the obligations created by medicine itself.

The standard focuses on patient status, continuity of care, clinical primacy, transparent economics, human accountability, universal-service duties, and excellence without extraction.

The medical industry exists in service of medicine.
Medicine does not exist in service of the medical industry.

======================================================================
13. NON-GOALS / GUARDRAILS
======================================================================

H.U.G.S. must not:
- Diagnose individual patients.
- Replace professional medical judgment.
- Become a partisan political platform.
- Assume that one country’s healthcare system is universally optimal.
- Treat all profit as inherently unethical.
- Treat all private healthcare as inherently unethical.
- Treat public ownership as inherently ethical.
- Collapse healthcare financing, delivery, licensing, and governance into one problem.
- Claim validated outcomes without evidence.
- Use aggregate scoring to conceal catastrophic failures.
- Permit algorithmic scoring to replace human accountability.

14. RESEARCH QUESTIONS
======================================================================

RQ-01:
Which healthcare functions should receive essential-service or common-carrier-like duties?

RQ-02:
What legal mechanisms can impose public-service obligations without requiring government ownership?

RQ-03:
What institutional structures best preserve clinical authority while maintaining cost discipline?

RQ-04:
How should physician and clinician excellence be measured without encouraging overtreatment or gaming?

RQ-05:
What constitutes an impermissible care gap?

RQ-06:
Who owns responsibility during hospital → rehab → home transitions?

RQ-07:
How should insurer denials be attributed to accountable human authority?

RQ-08:
Which financial incentives measurably increase encounter fragmentation?

RQ-09:
What minimum transparency should apply to ownership, pricing, referrals, and utilization management?

RQ-10:
How should H.U.G.S. distinguish resource scarcity from extractive denial?

RQ-11:
How should universal-service floors be defined across emergency, chronic, preventive, rehabilitative, mental health, pharmaceutical, and end-of-life care?

RQ-12:
How can the standard operate across public, nonprofit, private, and mixed systems?

15. INITIAL IMPLEMENTATION TEST CASES
======================================================================

CASE A — PRIMARY CARE FRAGMENTATION
Patient visits a multi-provider practice.
Different clinicians handle successive interactions.
No clinician owns longitudinal responsibility.
Automated scheduling and billing continue normally.
H.U.G.S. question:
Did the system preserve a coherent clinical trajectory or merely process encounters?

CASE B — HOSPITAL TO REHAB
Hospital treatment succeeds.
Patient transfers to rehabilitation.
Hospital physicians cease meaningful follow-through.
Rehab team possesses incomplete context or different incentives.
H.U.G.S. question:
Was responsibility formally transferred, accepted, and verified?

CASE C — INSURANCE DENIAL
Clinician recommends treatment.
Insurer denies based on administrative rule.
H.U.G.S. question:
Who exercised the override authority, what clinical risk was accepted, and what accountable appeal path existed?

CASE D — AUTOMATED ACCESS FAILURE
Scheduling or authorization software blocks timely access.
No human owns the resulting delay.
H.U.G.S. question:
Can an automated system create a clinically consequential outcome without a human authority trail?

CASE E — EXCELLENCE
Highly skilled clinician performs unusually difficult work with superior outcomes.
H.U.G.S. question:
How can the system materially reward this excellence without rewarding volume, fragmentation, or unnecessary intervention?

19. CANONICAL CLOSING ANCHOR
======================================================================

H.U.G.S. begins before the argument over who pays for healthcare.

It begins by asking what healthcare is.

Its answer is that medically necessary care creates obligations that cannot be adequately described by an ordinary customer-vendor relationship.

A healthcare system may contain markets.
It may contain private institutions.
It may reward exceptional professionals.
It may employ technology, automation, insurers, investors, public agencies, nonprofits, and mixed economic structures.

But those mechanisms remain subordinate to the patient.

The patient is not the product.
The illness is not the opportunity.
The encounter is not the mission.

The mission is care.
